THE THESIS

Riyadh has the
appetite. It is

Riyadh is the fastest-growing capital in the Gulf, with ~7.7M residents, ~13.4M non-Saudis across the Kingdom and a foodservice market on track for USD 48B by 2030. Yet there is no clear 5-star Shinwari / Pashtun destination serving its affluent Saudi, Pakistani, Afghan and GCC families.

Dubai's Wakha has already proven, across five branches, that elevated Pashtun fire-cuisine is a destination asset — not a casual category. Wakha Riyadh brings that proof point home, as a 5-star cultural hospitality flagship.

SHINWARI FIRE-CUISINE · 5 BRANCHES · DESTINATION MODEL

Wakha has scaled across Dubai as a destination Pashtun concept — open fire kitchens, meat theatre, communal hospitality and a strong cross-cultural guest mix of Emirati, Pakistani, Afghan, Indian and Western diners.

It demonstrates, with five operating branches, that elevated Shinwari cuisine commands destination footfall, repeat visitation, and premium ticket sizes — not the casual ceiling assumed by the category.

The implication for Riyadh: the model travels. A 5-star reinterpretation, built for Riyadh's affluent Saudi and GCC family base, can own a category that Dubai has already validated.
